How Synkopa works

A song in Synkopa is built from four things: channels make sound, patterns hold notes, the Playlist arranges patterns into a song, and the Mixer shapes what you hear. Read this page once and every other guide will make sense.

The four building blocks

If you have used FL Studio, this is the same model. If you come from a track-based DAW such as Ableton Live or Logic, read this section carefully: here a pattern is not tied to one track or one instrument.

Channelan instrument→ Patterna short piece of music→ Clipon the Playlist→ Mixer insertfader + effects→ Masterwhat you hear
Channel
One instrument: a sample (a kick, a clap), the built-in Synthesizer, a sampled piano, or a VST3 plugin. All channels live in the Channel Rack and belong to the whole project.
Pattern
A short piece of music: steps and notes for any of the channels. A drum loop and a bass line can be one pattern or two. Every pattern can use every channel.
Clip
A pattern placed on the Playlist, the song timeline. Ten clips of one pattern all play that pattern, so editing the pattern changes all ten. Audio files and automation curves are clips too.
Mixer insert
A strip on the Mixer with a fader, a pan knob and a chain of effects (FL Studio calls it a mixer track). Each channel plays into one insert.
Master
The last insert. Every other insert ends here, and its effects (a Limiter in a new project) process the whole song.

The surprise for newcomers: a pattern has no fixed place in the song. You decide where and how often it plays by placing clips on the Playlist.

The five windows

Each building block has its own window. The function keys open them, as in FL Studio.

WindowWhat you do thereOpen it
Channel RackAdd channels and click in drum steps for the current patternF6
Piano rollWrite notes with pitch and length for one channelF7, or right-click a channel → Piano roll
PlaylistArrange clips into a song, add markers and automationAlways open; F5 brings it to the front
MixerBalance levels, add effects, route and sidechainF9
BrowserFind patterns, audio and the sample libraryAlt + F8

The Channel Rack, Piano roll and Mixer can float or dock to an edge: drag a window's title bar onto one of the arrows that appear. View → Window layout → Reset window layout puts everything back.

PAT and SONG: what Space plays

The PAT / SONG switch in the toolbar decides what Space plays. Press L to flip it.

  • PAT loops the current pattern only. Use it while you write a beat or a melody.
  • SONG plays the Playlist. Clicking the Playlist's ruler or grid switches to SONG for you.

If Space plays something you did not expect, check this switch first.

Adjust Main volume in the toolbar by dragging the knob up or right to raise it, down or left to lower it. Hold Ctrl or Shift for fine adjustment. Double-click to return to 0 dB. Main volume sets how loudly you hear playback and previews on your device.

The current pattern

There is always one current pattern. It is the one the Channel Rack and the Piano roll show, the one PAT plays, and the one the Playlist's Draw tool places.

  • Change it with the pattern selector in the toolbar, the number pad (1–9, +, −), or by clicking a pattern clip.
  • Press F4 to start a new, empty pattern.
  • Press F2 to rename the current pattern.

How a sound reaches the speakers

Each row in the Channel Rack has a small box between its knobs and its name. It shows the mixer insert that channel plays into.

  • A number: the channel always plays through that insert. The starter drums use inserts 1–4.
  • A dash (Like clip track): the channel plays through the insert of the Playlist track its clip sits on. New synths and instruments start this way.
  • Double-click the box to give the channel a free insert of its own, named after it.

Why it matters: a sound needs its own insert before you can give it its own volume fader and its own effects. Two sounds that share an insert are mixed together.

Every insert ends in the Master. Inserts can also send to other inserts, for example all drums into a drum bus. Mix and route shows how.

Undo, save and recover

  • Ctrl + Z undoes any edit; Ctrl + Y redoes it. Edit → Undo names the step it will undo.
  • Ctrl + S saves a .synkopa project. Each save keeps the previous file as a backup next to it.
  • Ctrl + N saves a new version beside the current file (Beat_2, Beat_3…), so you can try an idea without losing the old one.
  • Autosave keeps a recovery copy every few minutes. If Synkopa closes unexpectedly, it offers that copy the next time it starts.

Find any command

  • Ctrl + K opens Find a command. Type what you want to do (export, mixer, quantize) and pick it from the list, with its shortcut.
  • ? shows the keyboard shortcuts.
  • The status line under the menus tells you what the control under the mouse does.
