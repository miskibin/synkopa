Edit audio clips

Place audio on the Playlist Trim, split and fade Fit a loop to the grid or reverse it Play chops from the Sampler

Place audio on the Playlist

Start with one loop or recording. Drag an audio file from your file manager, or drag a sample from the Browser, onto the Playlist track and beat where you want it to start. In SONG mode, click the ruler at the start of the clip and press Space to check it.

To follow the screenshots with a bundled example, choose File → Open demo project and locate the first clip on the Dark Vox track. The clip menu opens from its small top-left icon or by right-clicking its name.

Trim, split and fade

  1. Choose Trim in the Playlist’s Edges control. Drag a clip’s left or right edge to change the region it plays. Drag the body to move the clip. The audio on disk stays intact.
  2. Choose Slice from the Playlist toolbar and click inside the clip. In the example, cut at bar 3. Drag up or down to cut across multiple tracks at the same position. Hold Alt to cut without snap. Switch back to Draw when you are done.
  3. Hover at the clip’s upper left or right, just below its name. Drag the fade handle toward the middle of the clip for a fade in or fade out. If you cannot grab the handle, zoom in so the clip is wider, or use the FADE IN and FADE OUT controls in the Sample editor.

Choose Trim to the part that plays in the clip menu when you need a new file containing only the used region. This saves a separate copy and leaves the source intact.

For a crossfade between comp takes, use the Crossfade setting in the comp clip’s menu. Right-click a boundary in the comp to set only that transition. For two ordinary audio clips, shape their individual fades and listen to the overlap.

Fit a loop to the grid or reverse it

  1. Place the clip’s start on the beat you want. Choose Stretch under Edges, then drag its right edge to the end of the desired bar. Use Shift+M to switch between Trim and Stretch.
  2. Double-click the clip to open the Sample editor. Under How a stretch plays, choose Keep pitch to change speed while retaining pitch, or Resample to change speed and pitch together. Use Fit to when you know the exact length in beats or bars.
  3. Use Reverse to play the audio backward. If several clips share the source and you want separate sound settings, open the clip menu and choose Make unique first.
  4. Loop the region and listen for the start and end transients. If pitch-preserving rendering fails, Synkopa can play the stretch resampled. Check the clip menu for the error before exporting. The Warp mixer effect reshapes playback time or level over a bar and can be added after you have fitted the clip to the grid.

Play chops from the Sampler

  1. Open the audio clip’s menu and choose Add as sampler channel. Open its channel in the Channel Rack and select Slice.
  2. Choose Transients for hits or Equal for regular divisions. Click a slice to audition it. Drag a marker to move it, or double-click to add or remove one.
  3. Play from the sample’s root note upward to trigger slices in order. Use Dump to pattern to write one note per slice into the current pattern at the original timing, then rearrange the notes in the Piano roll.

The Playlist Slice tool divides an arrangement clip on the timeline. The Sampler’s Slice mode turns one file into several playable keys.
