Edit audio clips

Shorten a recording or sample, cut it into pieces, fade it in and out, make a loop fit your tempo, or chop it into hits you can play from the keyboard. None of these edits change the audio file on disk, so you can always go back.

Put audio on the Playlist Shorten, cut and fade a clip Fit a loop to the tempo Chop a sample into playable hits

Put audio on the Playlist

An audio file on the Playlist becomes an audio clip: a window onto the file that you can move, shorten and shape.

  1. Drag a WAV, MP3, FLAC, OGG or AIFF file from your file manager, or a sample from the Browser, onto a Playlist track at the beat where it should start.
  2. Click the ruler at the start of the clip and press Space.The clip plays with the rest of the song.

Every clip has a menu: right-click its name, or click the small icon at its top left. To follow the pictures below, choose File → Open demo project and find the first clip on the Dark Vox track.

Shorten, cut and fade a clip

Use these to remove a count-in or noise before the music, to split a recording into parts you can move separately, and to avoid clicks where a clip starts or stops abruptly.

Shorten a clip

  1. Make sure Edges above the tracks is set to Trim.
  2. Drag the clip's left or right edge inward.The clip plays less of the file. Drag the edge back out to get the rest back.

Drag the middle of a clip to move it.

Cut a clip in two

  1. Choose the Slice tool in the Playlist toolbar.
  2. Click inside the clip where you want the cut, for example at bar 3.Two clips, one on each side of the cut.
  3. Switch back to the Draw tool.

Drag up or down with the Slice tool to cut every track the line crosses at the same point. Hold Alt to cut off the grid.

Fade in or out

A fade raises or lowers the volume gradually at the start or end of a clip.

  1. Point at the top left or top right corner of the clip, just below its name.A small fade handle appears.
  2. Drag the handle toward the middle of the clip.A curve shows the fade.

Can't grab the handle? Zoom in so the clip is wider, or set FADE IN and FADE OUT in the Sample editor (double-click the clip).

  • Need a file with only the part that plays? Choose Trim to the part that plays in the clip menu. It saves a new copy; the original file stays untouched.
  • Blending two clips: overlap them on the track and give each a fade. Between comp takes, use the comp's Crossfade instead (see Record audio).

Fit a loop to the tempo

A loop recorded at a different tempo drifts against your beat. Stretching makes it play faster or slower so it fills exactly the bars you want.

  1. Move the clip so it starts on the right beat.
  2. Set Edges to Stretch, or press Shift + M.
  3. Drag the clip's right edge to the end of the bar where the loop should end.The clip plays faster or slower to fill that length.
  4. Press Shift + M to switch the edges back to trimming.
  5. Loop the clip and listen to the first and last beats.

A clip can stretch from a quarter to four times its speed. Comp clips cannot be stretched.

Choose how a stretch sounds

Double-click the clip to open the Sample editor. Under How a stretch plays:

  • Keep pitch changes only the speed. Use it for vocals and melodic loops.
  • Resample changes speed and pitch together, like a record played faster or slower.

If you know the length you want, pick it under Fit to (1 or 2 beats, or 1 to 8 bars) instead of dragging. Reverse plays the audio backward.

The Sample editor's settings belong to the sound, so every clip of the same file changes with them. To change one clip only, choose Make unique in its menu first.

A Keep pitch stretch is rendered in the background; until it is ready, the clip plays resampled. If rendering fails, the clip menu shows Stretch failed — plays resampled. Check for it before you export.

For rhythmic speed effects such as a tape stop, add the Warp effect to the clip's insert after fitting it.

Chop a sample into playable hits

Chopping cuts one recording, such as a drum break or a vocal phrase, into short pieces called slices, each on its own key. You can then replay the pieces in a new order from the Piano roll or a keyboard.

  1. Open the audio clip's menu and choose Add as sampler channel.A new channel with the sample appears in the Channel Rack.
  2. Click the channel's name in the Channel Rack to open its settings, then select Slice.
  3. Choose Transients to cut at every hit, or Equal to cut into equal parts. A transient is the sharp start of a sound, such as a drum hit.
  4. Click a slice to hear it.The slice plays from the sampler.
  5. Fix the cuts: drag a marker to move it, double-click to add or remove one.
  6. Click Dump to pattern.The current pattern gets one note per slice, in the original timing, so it plays the sample back as it was.
  7. Open the pattern in the Piano roll and move the notes to make a new rhythm.

The root note plays slice 1; each key above it plays the next slice.

Don't confuse the two slice features: the Playlist's Slice tool cuts a clip on the timeline, while the Sampler's Slice mode turns one file into many playable keys.
