EQ and dynamics

These effects fix the two most common mix problems: a sound whose tone is wrong (muddy, harsh, dull) and a sound whose level is uneven (words that disappear, hits that jump out, noise in the gaps). Each section says when to use the effect, gets you to a useful setting in a few steps, then explains the controls.

EQ or dynamics? Parametric EQ Compressor FET Compressor Gate De-esser Vocal Rider Multiband Limiter

EQ or dynamics?

Ask whether the problem is there all the time or only at certain moments.

  • Always there (every snare hit rings, the voice always sounds boxy): it is a tone problem. Use an EQ, which turns frequency ranges up or down.
  • Only sometimes (a few words too loud, some too quiet, noise between phrases): it is a level problem. Use a dynamics effect, which turns the sound up or down as it plays.

Whatever you add, compare it with and without the effect (the round power button at the top left of every effect window) at the same loudness. A louder version always seems better at first.

dB
Decibels, the unit for level. +6 dB is clearly louder, −1 dB is a small change.
Hz
Frequency, from low (bass around 50–200 Hz) to high (cymbal shimmer around 10 kHz).
Gain reduction (GR)
How many dB a dynamics effect is turning the sound down right now. Most windows show it in red.
Two snare problems: which tool for each?

If every snare hit has the same ring, start with a narrow Parametric EQ cut. If the tone is good but a few hits jump forward, start with Compressor and set the threshold so the gain reduction meter moves only on those hits.

Parametric EQ

Use it when a sound is muddy, boomy, boxy, harsh or dull, or a single frequency rings.

Quick start: clean up a muddy piano or guitar

  1. Add Parametric EQ to the insert and play the sound.The graph shows the sound before the EQ in grey and after it in color. The bands start flat.
  2. Drag the band point near 250 Hz down by 3–4 dB.
  3. Scroll the mouse wheel over the point to make the band wider.
  4. Turn the EQ off and on to compare.With the cut, the sound is clearer but should still have body. If it turns thin, cut less.

Controls

Drag a point
Sideways changes the frequency, up and down changes the gain.
Q
The width of a band. Scroll over a point to change it: a higher Q is narrower. Cut narrow, boost wide: a narrow boost tends to sound harsh.
Band shapes
A Bell changes the frequencies around one point. A Low shelf or High shelf changes everything below or above it. A Low cut removes everything below its frequency (also called a high-pass filter), a High cut everything above. Right-click a point to change its shape.
Add a band
Double-click empty space on the graph for a new bell. To catch a ringing frequency, drag down from the highlighted peak in the spectrum: a narrow cut appears exactly there.
Channel
Right-click a point to make it act on both sides, left, right, M (mid: what is in the center) or S (side: what is only in the stereo spread).

Cut only when it gets loud (dynamic band)

If a frequency is harsh only on loud notes, a fixed cut dulls the quiet ones too. A dynamic band moves only while its range is loud.

  1. Right-click the band's point and choose Dynamic: on.
  2. Below the graph, set RANGE to a negative value: how far it may cut.
  3. Lower THRESH until the band moves on the harsh notes only.The graph animates the band as it cuts.

Once an EQ has a dynamic band, a Sidechain box appears: choose another insert, and the band dips only when that insert is loud. The mix assistant sets this up for you.

Compressor

Use it when a sound's level jumps around (a vocal with loud and soft words, an uneven bass), when a drum bus should sound like one kit, or to duck the bass under the kick. A compressor turns the loud moments down, so the whole sound can sit louder and steadier.

Quick start

  1. Add Compressor to the insert and play the sound.
  2. Lower Thresh until the red GR meter shows 3–6 dB on the loudest parts.
  3. Set Ratio between 2:1 and 4:1 for gentle control.
  4. Raise Makeup by about the amount of gain reduction.
  5. Turn the effect off and on to compare.At the same loudness, the sound is steadier and the quiet parts are easier to hear.

Faster start: the presets Vocal — leveling and Drum bus — glue are good first settings.

Controls

Thresh
Threshold: the level where compression starts. Only the sound above it is turned down. Lower means more of the sound gets compressed.
Ratio
How strongly it turns down what goes over the threshold. At 4:1, a sound 4 dB over comes out only 1 dB over.
Attack
How fast it reacts. A longer attack lets the first crack of each hit through, so drums keep their punch. A shorter one catches it.
Release
How fast it lets go after the sound drops. Too long and it is still turning down at the next hit; too short and the level audibly pumps.
Makeup
Turns the whole result up again to make up for the reduction.
Sidechain
What it listens to: Own input, or another insert, such as the kick for ducking.
Detect (Advanced)
Peak follows fast hits; RMS follows the body of the sound, smoother for vocals.
Try threshold and ratio

Steady signal example before Makeup. Attack and Release affect when you hear the change.

The snare sounds soft even though the meter shows only a few dB.

Lengthen Attack while looping the same hit. When the crack returns, match the level with Makeup and compare again. If gain reduction is still active at the next hit, shorten Release.

FET Compressor

Use it when a vocal should jump forward and sound upfront, or room drums should be squashed for an aggressive sound. FET is the second type of the Compressor, modeled on the classic 1176: very fast, and it adds a little color as you push it.

Quick start

  1. In the Compressor window, click FET next to the effect name, or pick a preset starting with FET · in the effect picker.Switching type loads the FET's default settings.
  2. Raise Input until the needle shows 3–7 dB of gain reduction on the loud words or hits.
  3. Lower Output until bypassing the effect does not change the loudness.
  4. Compare with and without it.The vocal sounds closer and more even; room drums sound bigger.

Controls

Input
How hard you drive the sound into the compressor. The FET has no threshold knob: more Input means more compression.
Ratio
4, 8, 12, 20 or All. All is the famous "all buttons in" mode: aggressive pumping and distortion, for drums and rooms rather than a lead vocal.
Attack, Release
As on the Compressor, but much faster: attack is in microseconds.
Output
The level after compression.
Mix
Blends in the uncompressed sound. 30–50% gives parallel compression: the density of the compressed sound with the punch of the original.

Click the meter to switch it between gain reduction (GR) and output level (OUT).

Gate

Use it when you hear room noise, hum or other instruments in the gaps between notes, phrases or drum hits. A gate turns the sound down whenever it is quieter than the threshold. It does not remove noise during the notes: for steady noise under the words, use Denoiser.

Quick start

  1. Add Gate to the insert and play a part with gaps.The window draws the level as it plays; the lamp shows open, hold or closed.
  2. Raise Thresh until it sits just above the noise in the gaps.The gaps go quiet.
  3. Set Floor to about −14 dB, so some room sound stays and the silence does not sound unnatural.
  4. If the ends of notes or words are cut off, lengthen Release or Hold, or lower Thresh.

Controls

Thresh
The level that opens the gate.
Floor
How far the gate turns the sound down when closed. −∞ is silence.
Ratio
How hard it closes. ∞ is a hard gate; lower values turn quiet parts down gradually, which sounds more natural on vocals.
Hysteresis, Hold
Stop the gate from chattering (flickering open and closed) on a fading note: it closes only once the level is this far below Thresh, and stays open this long.
Attack, Release
How fast it opens and closes.
Lookahead
Lets the gate open a few ms before each hit, so drum attacks are not cut. The mixer keeps everything in time.
Mode
Duck turns the sound down while the detector hears something, for example music under a voice-over.
Sidechain
Let another insert open the gate, for example a hi-hat chopping a pad in rhythm.

De-esser

Use it when a vocal's S, SH and T sounds sting. The De-esser turns them down only at the moments they happen, so the rest of the voice stays bright. Put it after the vocal's EQ and compressor.

Quick start

  1. Add De-esser to the vocal and loop a phrase with sharp S sounds.
  2. Set Listen to Only S.You hear only the part being removed.
  3. Move Freq until you hear mainly the S sounds and no vowels. Male voices are often around 5 kHz, female voices around 8 kHz.
  4. Set Listen back to Off.
  5. Lower Threshold until the S sounds get 3–6 dB of reduction and the rest of the vocal gets none.

Controls

Threshold
How loud the S range must get before it is turned down.
Freq
Where the S range starts; the De-esser listens from there up.
Mode
Split turns down only the highs during an S, leaving the body of the voice alone. Wide turns down the whole voice for a moment.
Range
The most an S can be turned down. Lower it if the voice starts to lisp.
Lookahead
Lets it react before the S arrives, so the start of each S is caught.
The S sounds are calmer, but the whole voice dips with them.

Switch from Wide to Split at the same threshold and range. Split reduces only the highs, so the vowels keep their level. If the S itself sounds unnatural, lower Range.

Vocal Rider

Use it when some words in a vocal are much quieter than others. It works like an engineer riding the fader: it raises quiet words and lowers loud ones toward the level of their phrase, slowly enough to sound natural. It does not catch fast peaks, so a compressor after it is still useful.

Quick start

  1. Add Vocal Rider to the vocal, before any compressor, and loop a phrase that changes level.
  2. Set Speed to Singing or Rap.
  3. Raise Amount until the words sit more evenly.The green line shows the rider turning the vocal up or down.
  4. Compare with and without it.Quiet words are easier to follow, without breaths jumping forward.

Controls

Amount
How much each word is moved toward the level of its phrase.
Phrase
How much whole phrases are also nudged toward the take's usual level. Keep it low to preserve verse and chorus dynamics.
Max boost, Max cut
The furthest it may turn a word up or down. Lower Max boost if breaths get too loud.
Look-ahead
How far ahead it listens. Short +0.3 s and Long +1.6 s ride more accurately but delay the sound by that much; Live adds no delay, for recording and monitoring.
Advanced
Auto target (on by default) finds the take's level for you; Noise floor and Gate keep it from lifting breaths and room noise; Output sets the final level.

Multiband

Use it when you want the dense, bright, in-your-face sound popular on synths, drums and vocals in electronic music (often called the "OTT" sound). It splits the sound into lows, mids and highs and compresses each one separately in two directions: downward turns loud parts down, upward lifts quiet details such as reverb tails, room sound and noise.

Quick start

  1. Add Multiband to a drum loop or synth.
  2. Turn Depth down low, then raise it slowly.Quiet tails and details come forward and the sound gets denser.
  3. If the noise and room take over, lower Upward. If the kick loses its impact, lower Downward.
  4. Match the level with Out gain and compare with and without it.

Controls

Depth
How much of the effect is blended in. Start low.
Time
How fast the compression reacts and lets go.
Upward, Downward
The strength of each direction.
In gain, Out gain
Level into and out of the effect. More In gain drives the bands harder.
Thresholds
Drag the lines in the display: orange for downward, blue for upward. Drag the dividers to move where the bands meet. Band levels and crossovers are also under Advanced.

Limiter

Use it when the Master clips, or the finished mix should be louder without any peak crossing a set level. A limiter is a very fast, very strong compressor that guarantees a maximum level. A new project already has one on the Master insert. For a full mastering chain, use Master instead.

Quick start

  1. Keep the Limiter as the last effect on the Master insert and loop the loudest part of the song.
  2. Leave Ceiling at −1 dBTP, the safe value for streaming.
  3. Turn Gain up slowly.The mix gets louder and the red GR meter shows how much the peaks are turned down.
  4. Stop while the mix still keeps its punch. A few dB of reduction on the loudest hits is usually enough.

If the kick and snare flatten or the mix starts to pump, back Gain off: more loudness has to come from the mix itself.

Controls

Gain
How hard the mix is pushed into the limiter. More gain is louder, with more reduction.
Ceiling
The highest level the output can reach, measured as true peak.
Advanced
Release and Auto release set how fast the level comes back after a peak (auto keeps dense mixes from pumping). Lookahead: longer is smoother, shorter keeps more snap. Stereo link at 100% keeps the stereo image steady. Output lowers the final level.
