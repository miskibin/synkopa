EQ & dynamics

Tone vs. level

Use EQ when the tone needs to change. Use dynamics when the level needs to change with the performance.

Loop a few bars. Toggle the effect’s power to compare with and without it. Match the listening levels before deciding which sounds better.

Choose a tool for two different snare problems

If every snare hit has the same ring, start with a narrow Parametric EQ cut. If the snare tone is good and only the loud hits jump forward, start with Compressor and set the threshold so the gain reduction meter moves on those hits.

Parametric EQ

Change the tone by cutting or boosting a frequency range. A bell affects frequencies around a point. A shelf changes one end of the spectrum. A high-pass filter cuts frequencies below its cutoff.

Double-click the graph to add a band. Drag it sideways for frequency and up or down for gain. Scroll over it to adjust Q. A high Q narrows the band. Right-click the point to change its shape.

Try it. On a piano or guitar, move a bell down around 250 Hz with a broad Q. Compare clarity and body before and after. If harshness appears only on a few notes, use Dynamic band with a negative RANGE and adjust THRESH until the cut moves on those notes.

Compressor

The Clean compressor turns down louder moments. Thresh sets where reduction begins. At a Ratio of 4:1, a steady input 4 dB above threshold comes out about 1 dB above it before Makeup gain. Attack sets how quickly the compressor reacts. Release sets how quickly it lets go.

Try it. Loop a drum bus. Lower Thresh until the red GR meter moves on hits. Compare short and long Attack settings. A longer attack leaves more of the initial hit. If the level audibly swells between hits, adjust Release. Match the bypass level with Makeup.

For kick-triggered ducking, open Clean Compressor on the bass insert and choose the kick insert from Own input. The kick triggers the gain reduction on the bass.

Try threshold and ratio

Steady signal example before Makeup. Attack and Release affect when you hear the change.

The snare goes soft even though the meter shows only a few dB.

Lengthen Attack while looping the same hit. When the leading crack returns, match the level with Makeup and compare bypass. If gain reduction is still active at the next hit, shorten Release and listen again.

FET Compressor

Choose FET in the Compressor window or select a FET preset in the picker. Switching types loads defaults. This 1176-style circuit reacts quickly and adds color when driven. Input drives the compressor harder and Output sets the listening level.

Try it. On room drums, raise Input until the meter shows reduction, then lower Output to compare fairly. Hear how the room comes forward. Blend some dry sound back with Mix if the hits lose impact. Compare 4 against All in the Ratio row at a matched level to hear the stronger effect on the room tone.

Gate

A gate turns down the signal below its threshold. It can quiet room noise between phrases. Noise still passes through while the gate is open.

Try it. Loop a vocal with gaps. Raise Thresh just above the room noise. Set Floor around −14 dB for a quieter gap with some room tone left. If the gate flutters, increase Hold or Hysteresis. If it chops off the ends of words, increase Release or lower the threshold.

For steady noise underneath the words, try Denoiser.

De-esser

Detects sharp consonants such as S and SH, then turns them down as they happen. Split mode lowers the high band. Wide mode lowers the whole signal when detection fires.

Try it. Loop a vocal with sharp esses. Choose Only S under Listen and move Freq until you hear the consonants clearly. Turn Listen off. Lower Threshold so reduction happens mainly on those sounds. Lower Range if the voice starts to lisp.

The singer’s S sounds are calmer, but the whole voice dips.

Compare Split and Wide at the same threshold and range. Split only reduces the high band, so it can preserve more of the vowel level. If the S itself sounds unnatural, lower Range.

Vocal Rider

Moves level like a fader. It raises quieter words and lowers louder ones around a target. This helps phrase-to-phrase balance. A compressor can still catch faster peaks.

Try it. Loop a vocal that changes level. Keep Auto target on and raise Amount until words sit more evenly. The green trace shows gain going up or down. Lower Max boost or raise Gate if breaths become too loud. Use Live under Look-ahead when monitoring. Compare an uneven phrase with bypass and listen for quiet words becoming clearer without breaths stepping forward.

Multiband

Splits the sound into low, mid, and high ranges. The dividers set where the bands meet. Downward reduces loud parts in each band. Upward lifts quieter parts, including room tone and noise.

Try it. On a drum loop, start with low Depth and Upward at zero. Raise Upward and listen for quieter cymbal tails and room sound coming forward. If those details take over, lower Depth or Upward. Use Out gain to match the bypass level. If you lose impact in the kick while lifting the high-band detail, lower Downward and compare again.

Limiter

Set a maximum output peak with Ceiling. The limiter looks ahead to reduce peaks before they cross that line. A compressor usually shapes a wider range of dynamics. A limiter near the end of a chain can catch the last fast peaks.

Try it. Loop the loudest part of the song on the Master insert. Start with Ceiling at −1 dBTP and turn up Gain until the red GR meter just catches peaks. Push it further to hear the change, then back off if kick and snare flatten or the mix pumps.
