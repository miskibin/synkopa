Record audio

Record a voice, a guitar or any other sound through a microphone or audio interface, then sing the part a few times and keep the best moments of each pass. Read the first two sections before your first session: they decide whether you hear yourself on time and whether the recording lands on the beat.

1. Connect your microphone 2. Hear yourself on time 3. Record a take 4. Keep the best parts of several takes 5. Tune and mix the vocal

1. Connect your microphone

Synkopa records one input of your audio interface, in mono. First tell it which device to listen to, which socket the microphone is in and which track the recording goes to.

  1. Plug the microphone into your audio interface and put on headphones. With speakers, the backing track would leak into the microphone.
  2. Choose File → Settings… → Audio. Set Input to your interface and Output to the device your headphones are plugged into.
  3. In the Playlist, right-click the header of the track you want to sing on and choose Record into this track.A red dot on the header shows that recordings go there.
  4. Right-click the round Record button in the toolbar. Tick Audio and untick Notes, so a MIDI keyboard does not record at the same time.
  5. Click Record, or press R.The button turns red and an IN meter appears in the toolbar.
  6. Back in Settings → Audio, set Input channel to the socket your microphone uses: Input 1 for the first one. The list fills only while Record or monitoring is on.
  7. Sing the loudest line of the song.The IN meter moves. If it reaches the top, turn down the gain knob on your interface.

Without Record into this track, a recording goes to the first track that is empty from the playhead on, or to a new track below the others.

The IN meter stays empty? Check the signal light on the interface, the socket you chose and that your operating system allows Synkopa to use the microphone. After changing the input, turn Record off and on again.

2. Hear yourself on time

Two things can feel wrong when you record: your voice in the headphones comes back late, or the take lands slightly after the beat. Both come from latency.

Monitoring
Hearing your own voice in the headphones while you record.
Latency
The short delay between a sound reaching the microphone and coming out of the headphones, or landing in the recording.
Buffer
The computer handles audio in small chunks. The buffer is the size of one chunk, in samples. A smaller buffer means less delay but more work for the computer; too small and playback crackles.

Choose one way to hear yourself

  • Through your interface. Many interfaces have a direct-monitor knob or switch. You hear yourself with no delay, but without Synkopa's effects. Leave Synkopa's monitoring off, or you hear a second, late copy of your voice.
  • Through Synkopa. Click the headphones button in the toolbar, or choose View → Monitor input. Your voice plays through the track you record on, with the effects on its mixer insert. Effects that add delay (Vocal Rider, Pitch Correct, Denoiser, Limiter, De-esser) are skipped while you monitor. The recording itself is always your clean input.

If your voice sounds late when monitoring through Synkopa, choose a smaller Buffer in Settings → Audio: 128 or 256 samples works for most recording. If you then hear clicks, or the Underruns or Input dropouts count rises, go one size up. Switch back to a larger buffer for mixing.

Make takes land on the beat

Your audio driver does not always report its full delay, so takes can land a few milliseconds late. Measure it once for each device and buffer setting.

  1. In Settings → Audio, click Measure latency.
  2. Hold the microphone close to a speaker or one headphone cup for about five seconds, in a quiet room.Synkopa plays a few clicks, records them and fills in Recording offset.
  3. If it reports an uncertain measurement, try again louder and closer.

Recording offset is how far each new take is moved; a positive value moves it earlier. Measure again after changing the device or the buffer.

3. Record a take

A take is one recorded pass of a part. Start with a single take of one phrase.

  1. Click the Playlist ruler a bar before the phrase.The playhead moves there and the toolbar switches to SONG.
  2. If part of the ruler is highlighted, a loop is set: double-click the ruler to clear it. With a loop, Synkopa records several takes instead of one.
  3. Optional: turn on 1 BAR in the toolbar for a bar of clicks before recording starts, and press Ctrl + M for the metronome.
  4. Press R to arm Record.The button is red and the status line says which track the recording goes to.
  5. Press Space and perform the phrase.
  6. Press Space to stop.A new audio clip appears on the track.
  7. Press R to turn Record off, then press Space to listen back. While Record is on, Space starts a new recording.

Replace one line while the song plays (punch in)

Punching in starts recording in the middle of playback, so you can redo one line without stopping the song.

  1. With Record off, press Space a few bars before the line.
  2. Press R just before the line.Recording starts at the playhead.
  3. Press R again after the line.Recording stops and the song keeps playing.

Take missing or silent? See recording problems.

4. Keep the best parts of several takes

Few singers get a whole line right in one pass. Comping (short for compiling) means recording the same part several times, then picking the best moment of each pass: the first words from take 2, the last word from take 3. The result is the comp. Synkopa keeps every take, so you can change your picks at any time.

Record several takes in a loop

  1. Drag across the Playlist ruler over the phrase, starting a little before it.The ruler highlights the loop.
  2. Press R, then Space.
  3. Perform the phrase each time the loop comes round, two or three times.
  4. Press Space to stop, then R to turn Record off.One comp clip holds a take for each pass.

Pick the best parts

  1. Double-click the comp clip.Take lanes open below it, one per take.
  2. Press Space. Click the small triangle on a take lane to hear that take instead of the comp. Click it again to go back to the comp.
  3. Click the name of the best take to use all of it.
  4. If another take has a better word, drag across that word in its lane.That part now plays in the comp. Each lane shows how much of it is used.
  5. Play the whole comp and listen at each join for a cut-off breath or a jump in level.
  • A join clicks or sounds abrupt: right-click the boundary and pick a longer Crossfade (a short blend between the two takes). Crossfade in the comp clip's menu sets it for every join.
  • Try another take for one section: point at it in the comp and press Ctrl + ↑ or Ctrl + ↓ to step through the takes.
  • Tidy up: Delete unused takes in the comp clip's menu removes takes the comp does not play.

Turn the comp into one clip

The pitch editor works on ordinary audio clips, so flatten the comp before tuning it. Flattening removes the takes, so keep a copy of the project that still has them.

  1. Press Ctrl + S to save the project with its takes.
  2. Choose File → Save as… and give the working copy a new name.
  3. Right-click the comp clip's name and choose Flatten comp.One audio clip replaces the comp on the same track. Ctrl + Z undoes it.
