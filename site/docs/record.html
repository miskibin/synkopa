Record audio

Set up your input Check timing and monitoring Record a take Combine takes from a loop Edit the vocal

Set up your input

  1. Connect the microphone to the interface and plug headphones into your playback device. In File → Settings… → Audio, select your Input and Output.
  2. In the Playlist, right-click the vocal track header and choose Record into this track. Right-click the round Record button and enable Audio. For a vocal pass, turn off Notes so keyboard input stays out of that recording.
  3. Click Record to arm. Return to Audio settings and choose Input channel for the socket the mic is plugged into. Recording and monitoring open the input so its channels appear. The selected channel is captured in mono.
  4. Sing the loudest phrase and check the IN meter. If the signal clips, turn down the interface input gain. If the meter stays blank, check the interface’s own signal light, the input socket and the system’s microphone permission for Synkopa. Re-arm after changing the input.

Check timing and monitoring

Choose one path for your headphones. For input through Synkopa, turn on View → Monitor input. With a vocal track armed, this follows its mixer routing. If you monitor directly from the interface, leave Synkopa monitoring off to avoid hearing a delayed copy.

If monitoring feels delayed, try a smaller Buffer in Audio. If you hear clicks or the dropout count rises, try a larger buffer. To correct where the recorded take lands, use Measure latency. Hold the mic near the speaker or headphone so it can pick up the test clicks. A confident measurement updates Recording offset. A positive offset moves newly recorded audio earlier. Test the timing again after changing devices or buffer settings.

Record a take

  1. Choose SONG and click the Playlist ruler at the start of the phrase. Double-click the ruler to clear an existing loop for one pass. Enable 1 BAR for a count-in if you want one.
  2. With Record armed and the IN meter moving, press Space, sing the phrase, then press Space again to stop. The recording lands on the armed track. Without an armed track, Synkopa finds the first track with room.
  3. Disarm Record before playing back. Listen through the phrase once with the backing. Use recording troubleshooting if the take is missing or silent.

To punch in while the song is playing, start playback with Record off. Click Record at the start of the line you want to record and click it again to end the take while playback continues.

Combine takes from a loop

  1. Drag across the Playlist ruler to set the loop around the phrase. Arm Record, start playback, and sing through two or three passes. Stop and disarm. Synkopa groups the passes into a comp.
  2. Double-click the comp to open the take lanes. During playback, click the triangle beside a take to solo-preview it in place. Click it again to return to the current comp.
  3. Click the name of the strongest take to use it in full. If another take has a better word or line, drag across that section of its lane. Play the whole comp and listen for changes in breath, level and tone at the joins.

If a join sounds sharp, right-click the boundary for a local Crossfade, or change Crossfade in the comp menu for the group. Before note-level editing, save the project with its takes, then use File → Save as… to make a working copy. In the copy, choose Flatten comp in the comp menu. This renders the chosen regions into one audio clip and removes the take group from the current project. Ctrl + Z undoes that step.

Take the recorded or flattened audio clip into the vocal-production walkthrough to repair one word, try a reference melody, and export the result.
