Mix and route

Send a sound to a mixer insert

Route sounds to separate inserts to adjust their levels and effects independently.

  • Audio on a Playlist track → click the track’s mixer routing control or choose Mixer channel from its menu, then pick an insert.
  • Instrument in the Channel Rack → use the mixer channel control on the left of its row. Click to choose an insert. When the channel is following the clip track, double-click the control to give that channel a free insert of its own.

Open the Mixer with F9. The insert’s meter moves when its sound plays.

Balance levels

Balance a loop from the busiest section before adding processing. That gives you a stable reference when you compare effects.

  1. Switch to SONG and drag across the Playlist ruler to loop a section where the kick, bass, and lead all play. Play it and select those inserts in the Mixer with F9.
  2. Solo each insert briefly to check that its meter matches the sound you expect. Clear all solos. Bring the kick and bass into balance, then raise the lead just enough to follow the words or melody. Listen again with the rest of the instruments in.
  3. Watch the Master meter during the loudest hits. If it clips, lower the inserts feeding it and listen again. Check both the busy section and a quieter one before moving on.

If a fader also changes an unrelated sound, check which sources share the insert. Give the sound its own insert when you need separate processing or level moves.

Create a bus or effect send

Use a bus to share one fader or processor across several inserts. Use a parallel send when you want the original sounds and a shared wet effect at the same time.

  1. In the Mixer, use + to add an insert and rename it Drum Bus. Right-click the kick insert and choose Send to → Drum Bus. Do the same for the snare.
  2. Turn off the M switch on each source to route it through the bus alone. Keep M on for Drum Bus. Pull the bus fader down briefly. Both drums should get quieter together. If one stays loud, check for a direct route to Master.
  3. For a shared reverb, create a separate Reverb insert, load Reverb, and set the effect’s Mix to 100% and its slot Mix to 100%. Send a vocal or snare to it and leave each source’s M on so its dry sound still reaches Master.
  4. Select the source in the expanded Mixer and drag its send cable up or down to adjust the feed. Start quietly and listen at the end of a phrase. Lower the return fader to change the wet level for all sources together.

Add an effect

Build a short chain on one insert and compare it while that sound plays in the song. A vocal chain is a useful first example.

  1. Select the vocal insert. Choose Add effect… or press Shift + F9. Add Parametric EQ for a cut you can hear, then add Reverb to give the vocal space.
  2. Click a slot to open its effect. Choose a starting preset from the preset name in the window header. In Reverb, try a short Decay and Mix around 15%. The tail should be audible at the end of a phrase.
  3. Toggle the slot’s power to compare in context. Adjust the effect’s output to match levels when possible. Use the slot’s Mix control to blend any effect with its input. If Reverb already blends through its own Mix, leave the slot at 100% while you learn that knob.
  4. Drag slots up or down to change processing order. With EQ first, the signal entering the reverb already has the cut. With EQ last, it shapes the voice and the reverb together. Compare on the same phrase and keep the order that gives you the clearer tail.

Drag a slot onto another Mixer strip to move it. Hold Ctrl while dragging to copy it. To reuse the whole chain, right-click the insert and open FX chain preset → Save FX chain as preset…. On an empty destination insert, use Load FX chain preset… to recall it. The loader can also Append the preset after existing effects or Replace them. Check gain and sidechain sources on the new track.

Pick an effect by what you hear or change an effect as the song plays.

Make room when sounds compete

Listen for two parts that are clear on their own and crowded together. For example, loop a kick and 808 that play at the same time and check whether the kick loses definition.

  1. While the section plays, look for a masking dot on the insert that covers another. Click the dot to see the reported pair and frequency range. Listen to those sounds together and check that you hear the overlap before applying a fix.
  2. Choose Make room for the other insert. Synkopa adds a Parametric EQ at the end of the masking insert’s chain, with a dynamic band keyed by the other sound. Use Review existing when a keyed effect already handles the pair.
  3. Open the EQ and toggle bypass while both parts play. Check whether the covered sound is easier to hear and whether the other sound retains enough body. If the change is too strong, reduce the dynamic band’s cut. Undo the addition if the first balance worked better.

Choose Ignore this pair for intentional overlap. To switch off the assistant, use Settings → Editing.

Sidechain a compressor

Duck a bass around the kick. The Compressor on the bass receives the bass as its audio and follows the kick as its detection source.

  1. Route kick and bass to separate inserts. Play a section containing both and confirm each insert shows activity.
  2. Right-click the kick insert. Choose Sidechain to, then the bass insert. Synkopa connects the kick to a Clean Compressor on the bass, adding one if needed. Click the Compressor slot to check that its sidechain input shows the kick.
  3. While both sounds play, lower Thresh until the red GR meter moves on kick hits. Start with a short Attack and adjust Release so the bass recovers before the next kick. Toggle bypass and listen for the kick’s attack to come through. For subtler movement, use less reduction.

When the meter stays flat, confirm the source name and make sure the kick plays in that range. In the Compressor, use Own input or the current source name to open the source menu. Choose the kick insert there when wiring the Compressor directly.
