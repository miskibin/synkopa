Mix and route

Mixing makes every part of the song audible at the right level. This page shows how to give each sound its own fader, balance the levels, add effects, group sounds on a bus and keep the kick and bass from fighting. Start once the arrangement is mostly in place.

Give a sound its own fader

Every sound plays into a mixer insert: one strip in the Mixer with a fader, a pan knob and a chain of effects. Sounds that share an insert share its fader and effects. To change one sound on its own, it needs an insert of its own. How a sound reaches the speakers explains the idea.

  1. Press F9 to open the Mixer.The first strip is the Master: everything you hear passes through it.
  2. In the Channel Rack, find the small box between a channel's knobs and its name. It shows the insert that channel plays into. A dash means it follows the Playlist track its clip is on.
  3. Double-click the box.The channel gets a free insert of its own, named after it.
  4. Play the song.That insert's meter moves when the sound plays.

To pick an existing insert instead, click the box and choose one. For an audio clip on the Playlist, right-click the clip and choose Mixer channel…, or right-click the track name and open Mixer channel.

Many channels at once: Shift-click their names in the Channel Rack, select an insert in the Mixer, then press Ctrl + L to send them all there, or Shift + Ctrl + L to give each its own insert from that one on.

Balance the levels

Set the faders before you add effects. A good balance often fixes what you were about to fix with an effect, and it gives you a fair starting point to compare against.

  1. Switch to SONG and drag across the Playlist ruler over the busiest section, where the kick, bass and lead all play.That section loops.
  2. Pull every fader down a little, then bring up the kick and the bass until they sit well together.
  3. Raise the lead or vocal until you can follow it easily, then bring in the rest.
  4. Watch the Master meter on the loudest hits.If CLIP lights red above it, the mix is too loud: lower the faders that feed it, not just the Master.
  5. Loop a quieter section and check the balance there too.

Double-click a fader to reset it to 0 dB, or a pan knob to center it. M mutes a strip and S solos it; Ctrl-click or right-click M also solos.

If moving one fader also changes a sound you did not mean to touch, both sounds share that insert. Give one of them its own insert.

Add an effect

Effects change a sound: EQ shapes its tone, a compressor evens its level, reverb gives it space. Each insert runs its effects from top to bottom. Not sure which effect you need? Pick one by what you hear.

  1. Select the insert, for example the vocal.
  2. Press Shift + F9, or click Add effect under the insert's effect list.The effect picker opens. Type to search, or pick a category.
  3. Choose Reverb.Its window opens, already set to its first preset.
  4. Play a phrase and listen to the end of it.You hear the reverb tail after the last word.
  5. Click the dot on the effect's row in the Mixer to bypass it, then click again to turn it back on. Compare the two.

To try other settings, step through presets with ‹ and › at the top right of the effect window, or click the preset name for the full list.

Order matters. With an EQ before a reverb, the reverb receives the already-shaped sound. With the EQ after it, the EQ shapes the voice and its tail together. Drag an effect up or down to change the order.

Blend, move and reuse effects

  • Each effect row has a Mix control that blends the effect with the unprocessed sound (dry/wet: dry is the original, wet is the effect). Effects with their own Mix knob, such as Reverb, are easier to set there.
  • Drag an effect onto another strip to move it there. Hold Ctrl to copy it.
  • To reuse a whole chain, right-click the insert and choose FX chain preset → Save FX chain as preset…. On another insert, Load FX chain preset… brings it back: Append adds it after the effects already there, Replace swaps them out.

To change an effect as the song plays (a filter that opens in the chorus), see Automate controls.

Group sounds on a bus

A bus is an insert that other inserts feed, so one fader and one set of effects control them all: turn all the drums down at once, or compress them together. A send is the connection from one insert to another. Sends are taken after the source's fader.

  1. Click + after the last strip in the Mixer to add an insert.
  2. Double-click its name and call it Drum Bus.
  3. Right-click the kick's strip and choose Send to → Drum Bus. Do the same for the snare and the hats.
  4. Maximize the Mixer: double-click its title bar, or choose View → Maximize mixer.A lane of cables under the strips shows every send.
  5. Under each drum strip, beside the cable dot, click M to turn off its direct output to the Master. (This is not the mute button above the fader.)The drums now reach the Master only through the bus.
  6. Pull the Drum Bus fader down.All the drums get quieter together. If one stays loud, its M is still on.

Right-click a cable for Only here (not to the Master), which does the same as turning off M, or for Remove send. Drag a cable up or down to change how much is sent. Synkopa refuses a send that would loop back on itself.

Share one reverb between several sounds

Put a single reverb on its own insert and send several sounds to it. They sound like they are in the same room, and one fader sets the amount of reverb for all of them.

  1. Add an insert, name it Reverb and add the Reverb effect to it.
  2. Set the effect's Mix to 100%, so the insert carries only the reverb.
  3. Right-click the vocal's strip and choose Send to → Reverb. Leave the vocal's M on, so its dry sound still reaches the Master.
  4. Start with a low send level and raise it while listening to the end of a phrase.
  5. Use the Reverb insert's fader to set the overall amount for every sound you sent.

Fix two sounds that clash

Two parts can each sound fine alone and blur together, typically a kick and an 808 that are both loud around 50 Hz. This is called masking: one sound covers the other. While you play, the mix assistant listens for it and marks the insert that should make room with a small amber badge. Nothing changes until you click.

  1. Play the section where both parts sound together.
  2. Click the amber badge on the strip.A menu names the pair and the frequency, for example 808 covers Kick at 45–56 Hz.
  3. Listen to the two parts together and check that you hear the problem.
  4. Choose Make room for the other sound.Synkopa adds a Parametric EQ at the end of the strip's chain. Its one band dips that range only while the other sound plays.
  5. Bypass the new EQ and compare.The covered sound should come through more clearly, while the other keeps its body between hits.

If the dip is too strong, open the EQ and make its cut smaller. If you preferred it before, undo with Ctrl + Z. When the menu offers Review existing, an effect already ducks that pair: open it and adjust it instead.

Overlap on purpose? Choose Ignore this pair. To turn the assistant off, go to File → Settings… → General → Mix assistant.

Duck the bass under the kick

When the kick and bass hit together, the low end gets crowded. Ducking turns the bass down for a moment on every kick, so the kick punches through and the bass comes back between hits. It works through a sidechain: the compressor on the bass listens to the kick instead of to the bass.

  1. Make sure the kick and the bass each have their own insert.
  2. Right-click the kick's strip and choose Sidechain to, then the bass insert.The bass's Compressor now listens to the kick. If the bass had none, one set up for ducking is added and opens.
  3. Play a section with both.The red GR (gain reduction) meter moves on every kick, and the bass dips.
  4. For more ducking, lower Thresh. For less, raise it.
  5. Adjust Release so the bass comes back up before the next kick.

If the meter does not move, check that the kick plays in that section. In the Compressor window, the sidechain box (Own input or a source name) shows what it listens to; click it to pick the kick insert yourself. The Compressor section explains Thresh, Attack and Release.
