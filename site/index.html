The first comment under every post about Synkopa

“Another FL Studio clone. Why?”

Fair. The workflow is copied on purpose: patterns, Channel Rack, piano roll, playlist, down to right-click-deletes. I have made beats in FL Studio since 2010 and I did not want to relearn my hands.

The reasons to install it are the parts that are not copied. There are six. Each one is below with something you can click, hear or check.

First, the copied part

If you know FL Studio, you can already use this.

Click adds a step, right-click deletes it, Alt+wheel changes its velocity, Space plays. These are the same gestures as in the app, on TR-808 samples from its library.

Space · click · right-click · Alt+wheel

The dark strip is reason 3 arriving early. Those are real synkopa commands for the pattern you see. Edit a step string and the grid follows. In the app a terminal does the same thing to the project on your screen.

1
Reason one

Nothing is locked behind an edition.

FL Studio sells four editions, and the cheapest one cannot record audio. Synkopa has one build and no account.

2
Reason two

It runs on Linux. Natively.

FL Studio has no Linux build. The usual open-source answer for the FL workflow is LMMS, which does not record audio. Synkopa is that workflow on Linux with recording: ALSA and JACK, including PipeWire's JACK, VST3 instruments and effects, LV2 effects, packaged as deb, rpm and AppImage.

On Windows it speaks WASAPI and ASIO. On a Mac (Apple Silicon, macOS 13.4 or newer) it plays through Core Audio; VST3 plugins are not hosted there yet.

3
Reason three

A terminal can play it. So can an AI agent.

Synkopa installs a synkopa command that edits the project you have open. Notes, clips, effects and knobs change on your screen, and every command is one step in your undo history. An agent cannot hear, so it bounces offline and reads the numbers back.

$ synkopa context --pretty              # the song, as JSON
$ synkopa pattern --id 2 --ch hat --steps "x.x. x.x. x.x5 x.x9"
$ synkopa add-fx --insert 3 --type sat --preset 5
$ synkopa render --out check.mp3 --from 0 --to 32
{ "peakDbfs": -1.1, "rmsDbfs": -12.3, "clippedSamples": 0 }
$ synkopa watch song.txt                # the file is the song: save, and the loop changes
Nobody touched a mouse

4
Reason four

The effects show their work.

My day job is noise cancellation DSP for telecom radios. I wanted every built-in effect to draw what it does to the signal passing through it, with the same controls everywhere. These were captured while a beat played.

16 effects, a wavetable synth, a sampled grand piano and a sampler are built in. The audio thread does not allocate or lock, and a benchmark checks that on every change. How it is put together

5
Reason five

It does not make you wait.

I timed both apps on the same PC, the way you wait for them: from the key press or click to the moment the screen stops changing. FL Studio opened its demo song WiseLabs – Nahash; Synkopa opened a song generated to the same size: 89 channels, 82 patterns, about 5,500 notes, 440 playlist clips and 120 mixer effects.

Neither app dropped audio during ten minutes of editing while the song played at a 512-sample buffer. Synkopa renders instruments and mixer chains on every CPU core, and its output is bit-identical to a single thread.

Medians of 5 runs (3 for the effect, 60 moves for the drag) on Windows 11, Intel i5-12400F, 32 GB, 1920×1080 at 144 Hz; Synkopa 0.9.17 against FL Studio 20.7.2, 16 November 2026. The two songs are the same size, not the same sound: FL's Harmor patches cost more to load than Synkopa's synth presets. The other half of an honest answer

Do not install it if

  • you need your VST3 plugins on a Mac, or have an Intel Mac. The macOS build is Apple Silicon only and hosts no VST3 yet.
  • your work lives in .flp files or FL's own plugins. Neither opens here. Stems, samples and VST3 plugins do.
  • you write by launching clips. There is no Session view.
  • you need twenty-five years of polish tonight. FL Studio has it. This started in August 2026.
